What it does

A cancer type in, ranked designs out

You give it an indication and name no target. It screens all 3,466 human cell-surface proteins against tumour and normal-tissue evidence, ranks them, tests 19,900 two-antigen combinations, retrieves real antibody binders, chooses a receptor architecture from each target's safety profile, and assembles a complete DNA construct that must fit inside the viral vector.

Every number traces to a public dataset and a release date, and where a measurement is missing the platform says so rather than substituting one.

The result

The one thing outstanding

All eight are waiting on the same single part

These are adaptor designs. The T cell does not recognise the tumour directly — it recognises a small chemical tag, and a separately dosed protein carries that tag to the antigen. That is what makes them fit, and what makes them safer: the therapy works only while the second protein is being given, so it can be switched off.

The cell therefore needs a binder against the tag. Such binders are published, but none exists in any public antibody database the platform can query — zero of 21,914 structural entries. Rather than invent a plausible-looking sequence, the platform declares the part's exact size and assembles around it.

Practically: the eight designs are real and correctly sized. To order DNA, one sequence must be licensed or obtained from the published literature — a procurement and IP question, roughly a week of work, not a research problem. Everything downstream of it is finished.

The decision we need from you

How much stoppable risk is acceptable

An adaptor does not make an antigen safer — it makes the exposure stoppable. How much risk that justifies is a clinical judgement, not something derivable from data, so it is a declared number. Today it is 0.35, and this is what each setting would admit:

Confidence

108 of 114 checks clear, and the six that don't are on the record

Every stage is tested against criteria written before it runs. Six are currently failing, each deliberately and each documented:

  • A screening check was withdrawn — it compared two groups that differ by construction, so it could never pass however the data looked. A replacement was written and clears.
  • Tumour-overlap figures are confounded by gene length, so they are reported next to a length-matched percentile and excluded from choosing partners.
  • One accepted antigen pair includes an immune-system protein. It is reported rather than filtered out, because the candidate list is not hand-curated.
  • Half of accepted pairs would fail if an unmeasured antigen turned out to be highly expressed — the cost of refusing to guess.
  • Partner choice is unstable when the candidate pool is halved, so the pairing stage is marked complete-with-limitations.
  • Mesothelin does not reach an adaptor design, as described above. Left failing on purpose, pending your decision on the ceiling.
